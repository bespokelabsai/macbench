# Dubai International: one live day of air traffic control

Your team is the air traffic control unit at Dubai International (OMDB) for one day, starting at 18:00Z. Each of you is a controller. The weather comes with that day's forecasts. You have 4 hours. The day stops when your session ends. The day runs at three times real time: each hour of the run is three hours at the airport, so a 4-hour run covers 18:00Z to 06:00Z. The day starts at the team's first API call, or 10 minutes after your shift begins if nobody has called by then, and it never pauses.

You work the airport through its ATC system. Reach it with `POST $MULTI_AGENT_HARNESS_BOARD_URL/api/atc/<endpoint>` and the bearer token in `$MULTI_AGENT_HARNESS_BOARD_TOKEN`, with the MCP tools `atc_status`, `atc_query`, `atc_events` and `atc_act` on the `board` server when your run has one. Every instruction you send, however you send it, is held to the same limits: each sector's radio time and your seat's rate limit in `docs/API.md`. Read `docs/MANUAL.md` for the airport, the procedures and the rules, and `docs/API.md` for every query and instruction.

<a id="R01"></a>**R01: Positions and frequencies.** The airport is divided into these positions: Arrivals (ARR), Director (DIR), Tower North and South (TWR_N, TWR_S), Ground (GND, GND_2), Delivery (DEL), Departures North and South (DEP_N, DEP_S), flow management (FLOW) and supervisor (SUP). Each radio position has its own frequency. Handoffs between positions have standard conditions (MANUAL section 4). You work a sector by opening it with the `open` op, and you can work several. Two controllers cannot work the same sector. An aircraft talks to the sector it has been handed to, and only that sector's controller can instruct it. A sector nobody has opened has no controller. Its aircraft get no instructions or clearances: on the ground they wait, and in the air they hold until their fuel runs low and then divert.

<a id="R02"></a>**R02: Radio time.** Every instruction uses radio time for the transmission and the pilot's readback. Pilots' own calls use radio time too. A frequency carries one transmission at a time. The sectors you work are on separate frequencies, so you can transmit on all of them at once. An instruction takes effect when the readback ends. You hear pilots as words on the radio.

<a id="R03"></a>**R03: The score.** The score is the delay-weighted share of the day's flights that the airport handles, multiplied by a safety factor. It covers the flights scheduled up to 30 minutes before the grading time. The grading time of a mark is the same for every team: the airport time at 15 minutes of run time before the mark, which is 45 minutes of airport time (the 4-hour mark grades the day at 05:15Z). A day that stopped before its grading time scores 0 at that mark.

- A flight earns full credit when it lands (an arrival) or takes off (a departure) within 15 minutes of its scheduled time. Credit falls linearly to 0 at 4 hours 15 minutes late.
- A flight that has not landed or taken off by the grading time earns 0 at that grading.
- A diverted arrival earns credit only once its return flight lands (or, if that diverts too, the return flight after it), with the delay counted from the original flight's scheduled time. A cancelled or crashed flight earns 0.

Each safety event multiplies the score by its class factor (`docs/MANUAL.md` section 5). Accidents always count in full. All other events count per 100 flights graded: with N flights graded, their combined factor is raised to the power 100 / N, and counts in full while N is under 100. The score runs from 0 to 1.
